Parliament AI System — Legislative Intelligence

Three people, one bill, and the moments AI actually earns its place

A walk through how a Member of Parliament, a committee researcher, and the legislative oversight unit each move through the system — from the first blank page to the audit trail left behind.

Owner Parliament of Rwanda Technology partner MINICT / RISA Deployment Cloud-first → on-prem Languages Kinyarwanda · English · French

Persona · Member of Parliament / Legal Drafter

Uwase Diane, Legal Drafter, Chamber of Deputies

Assigned to turn a policy directive on digital data protection into a draft bill. Works mostly in Kinyarwanda and English, under a committee deadline, and is personally accountable for every clause she tables.

Trigger
Assigned a new bill to draft
Primary goal
A defensible, gap-free first draft
Success looks like
Draft tabled with no committee rework
Biggest fear
An unnoticed conflict with existing law

Confidence across the journey

1

Opens the brief, not a blank page

Human

Diane receives the policy directive and needs to understand what already exists in law before writing a single clause.

Pain today

Searching PDFs of past Gazettes by hand, no way to know if a topic is already covered elsewhere.

System role

None yet — she signs in, selects the bill workspace, and sets language to Kinyarwanda.

She leaves with

A scoped workspace for the bill, linked to the policy directive.

2

Researches with the drafting assistant

Research & Drafting Assistant

She asks the assistant to summarise every existing provision touching "data protection" and "digital governance," then drafts clause-by-clause with template filling.

Pain today

Hours lost to manual cross-referencing; easy to miss a relevant 2016 law.

System role

Retrieves and summarises grounded sources, every claim cited to a specific law and article; drafts sections from templates on request.

She leaves with

A first draft with inline citations she can verify, not blind trust.

"It doesn't write the bill for me. It stops me from writing it blind."

3

Runs the automated quality pass

Duplication Gaps & Inconsistency

Before showing anyone, she runs duplication detection and gap analysis against her own draft.

Pain today

Conflicts and vague clauses only surface in committee — in public, after the fact.

System role

Flags a clause that overlaps an existing law's Article 14, and marks two clauses as vague with suggested tightened language — all as suggestions.

She leaves with

A severity-tagged issue list she personally accepts or rejects, one by one.

4

Benchmarks against comparative legislation

Comparative Legislation

She checks how similar bills were framed elsewhere, to pre-empt a question she expects from committee.

Pain today

Comparative research is ad hoc, usually skipped under deadline pressure.

System role

Surfaces equivalent provisions from peer countries with a short AI takeaway on divergence — she treats it as a starting point.

She leaves with

Language ready for the "how does this compare" question.

5

Finalises and tables the bill

Human decision

Every accepted suggestion is hers by decision, not by default. She submits a draft she can defend clause by clause.

Pain today

Committee rework cycles because issues surface too late.

System role

Locks a version snapshot and records the full accept/reject trail against her name.

She leaves with

A tabled bill with fewer surprises — and an audit trail that protects her judgment.

Persona · Parliamentary Research & Committee Staff

Mugisha Eric, Committee Research Officer

Supports a standing committee. Receives tabled bills and must produce a briefing note before the committee sits — usually with two to three days' notice and several bills in flight at once.

Trigger
A bill is tabled to his committee
Primary goal
A briefing the committee can act on
Success looks like
No blind spots raised mid-session
Biggest fear
Missing a conflict a member later catches

Confidence across the journey

1

Bill lands on the committee queue

Human

Eric opens the newly tabled bill inside the shared workspace, already linked to the drafter's version history.

Pain today

Bills arrive as static documents with no context on what changed or why.

System role

Shows the version history and the drafter's own accepted/rejected AI suggestions as background context.

He leaves with

A head start instead of a cold read.

2

Deep-dives with the research assistant

Research & Drafting Assistant

He asks targeted questions — "what precedent exists for Article 9", "summarise committee reports on related bills since 2022" — building his own understanding fast.

Pain today

Research is the majority of prep time, most of it just locating documents.

System role

Answers grounded in the parliamentary knowledge base, cited to source, in Kinyarwanda or English as needed.

He leaves with

Verified context, hours faster than manual search.

3

Independently cross-checks the bill

Duplication Gaps & Inconsistency

He re-runs duplication and gap checks himself, as a second, independent pass — not just trusting the drafter's own review.

Pain today

No independent verification step; committee is the first real second opinion.

System role

Surfaces the same analytical layer independently, plus flags anything the drafter dismissed that he wants to re-examine.

He leaves with

Confidence the committee won't be blindsided.

"I'm not re-doing her job. I'm the second pair of eyes the process was missing."

4

Prepares and presents the briefing

Human decision

He drafts the committee briefing note, using the assistant to summarise findings into plain language, and presents it in session.

Pain today

Briefing notes assembled overnight from scattered notes.

System role

Drafts a summary from his findings for him to edit and own — never sent to committee unreviewed.

He leaves with

A tighter, better-substantiated briefing, delivered on time.

Persona · Legislative Quality & Oversight Unit

Legislative Quality Unit, Office of the Clerk

Doesn't draft bills — governs the system that helps draft them. Responsible for trust in the tool itself: that suggestions stay grounded, drafts stay confidential until tabled, and every AI action is traceable.

Trigger
Ongoing — daily monitoring, plus incidents
Primary goal
An auditable, trustworthy system
Success looks like
Zero ungrounded suggestions reach members
Biggest fear
A hallucinated citation nobody catches

Vigilance across the journey

1

Opens the oversight dashboard

Cross-cutting

Starts the day with a system-wide view: how many suggestions were made, accepted, rejected, and by whom, across every active bill.

Pain today

No comparable oversight function exists for AI-assisted drafting — this is a new discipline.

System role

Aggregates the audit log into acceptance-rate trends and highlights any suggestion overridden without comment.

Leaves with

A system-level read, not a bill-by-bill guess.

2

Spot-checks AI suggestions against source

Assistant Gaps

Samples a set of recent suggestions and manually verifies the cited article actually says what the system claims.

Pain today

N/A — this verification step doesn't exist without the audit trail and citations to check against.

System role

Every suggestion is stored with its exact source citation, making spot-checks fast rather than forensic.

Leaves with

Either confirmation, or a specific citation to escalate.

3

Investigates a flagged risk item

Escalation

A drafter reports a suggestion that looked wrong. The unit traces exactly what was retrieved, what was generated, and who saw it.

Pain today

Without full lineage, "the AI was wrong" is unactionable.

System role

Full request/response/citation lineage is retrievable per suggestion — the fix (retrieval gap, translation error, stale document) is diagnosable.

Leaves with

A root cause, not a shrug.

"If we can't explain a suggestion after the fact, it shouldn't have been made in the first place."

4

Reports to leadership, feeds improvement

Human decision

Compiles findings into a periodic report to Parliament leadership and MINICT/RISA, informing the on-prem migration and roadmap priorities.

Pain today

No feedback loop from usage back into system priorities.

System role

Provides the underlying metrics; the judgment and recommendation stay human.

Leaves with

Evidence-based input into what gets built or hardened next.
